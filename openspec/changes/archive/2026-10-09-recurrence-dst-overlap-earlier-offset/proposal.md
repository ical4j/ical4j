## Why

When a recurrence instance falls in the hour that repeats at a daylight-saving fall-back transition, `Recur<ZonedDateTime>.getDates` returns it with the *later* offset (e.g. `2019-11-03T01:30-08:00` for America/Los_Angeles) whenever the candidate happens to be derived from a seed carrying the standard-time offset. RFC 5545 §3.3.5 says such a local time denotes the first occurrence, i.e. the offset in force before the transition (`-07:00`). 3.x got this right; 4.x's result depends on `WKST` and on the month the seed falls in (issue #716).

## What Changes

- Every candidate `Recur` produces for a `ZonedDateTime` seed is normalised with `ZonedDateTime.withEarlierOffsetAtOverlap()` before it is returned from `getCandidates`, which feeds both `getDates` and `getNextDate`. Gaps (spring-forward) are unaffected: java.time already shifts a non-existent local time forward by the gap length and that is retained.
- `AbstractDateExpansionRule.withTemporalField`, `Recur.increment` and the individual BY* rules are not changed individually: all of their output flows through `getCandidates`, so one normalisation covers the BYDAY expansion (the reported case), the plain seed-increment path (daily rule seeded in standard time) and any future rule.
- **Behaviour change**: instances in a fall-back overlap now always carry the pre-transition offset, which is one hour earlier as an instant than before for the affected cases. Only `ZonedDateTime` recurrences are affected; `Instant`, `OffsetDateTime`, `LocalDateTime` and `LocalDate` seeds are untouched.

## Impact

- `src/main/java/net/fortuna/ical4j/model/Recur.java` — `getCandidates` return path plus a private helper; no signature changes.
- New `src/test/groovy/net/fortuna/ical4j/model/RecurDstOverlapSpec.groovy`.
- Consumers computing instances across a fall-back transition for `ZonedDateTime` recurrences will see the overlap instance one hour earlier (as an instant) than 4.0–4.4 produced in the affected cases; this matches 3.x and RFC 5545.
